Analysis
Key Findings
What the data tells us about urban mobility patterns in New York City.
2.3×
Summer Surge
Trip volume peaks in August at 4.0M — more than double February's 1.7M floor. The May–October period accounts for 63% of all 2023 ridership, signaling strong weather sensitivity despite a large year-round member base.
8am / 5pm
Commuter-First Network
The dual rush-hour peaks (8am: 2.1M trips, 5pm: 3.2M trips) and weekday dominance confirm Citi Bike primarily serves daily commuters. Wednesday and Thursday are the two highest-volume days of the year.
81%
Strong Membership Loyalty
More than 4 in 5 trips are taken by annual members. The membership rate stays elevated even in summer when casual tourist demand is highest, suggesting a deep, habitual user base beyond seasonal visitors.
50% → 62%
Electric Inflection Point
Electric bikes reached exact parity with classic bikes in January and continued climbing, finishing December at 62%. This trend likely reflects both ongoing fleet expansion and rider preference — e-bikes enable longer trips and faster speeds.
67%
Hyper-Short Trips
Two-thirds of all rides are completed in under 15 minutes. Combined with the commuter signal, this positions Citi Bike as a last-mile connector rather than a recreational vehicle — bridging subway stops, offices, and residential blocks.
~96K
Daily Ride Baseline
Averaging 96,000 trips per calendar day across the year — and over 128,000 per day in August — Citi Bike has embedded itself as a genuine transit mode in NYC, not merely a tourism amenity. The system is now structurally load-bearing for urban mobility.
